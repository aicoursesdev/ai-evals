A little care after each trip keeps your backpack waterproof for years.

## After every trip

- **Empty it** and shake out sand and grit; grit wears the zips.
- **Rinse with fresh water** after the beach or a boat. Salt dries into crystals that stiffen the fabric and corrode buckles.
- **Dry it open, in the shade.** Hang it upside down with the zips and roll-top open. Strong sun for days on end fades the fabric and weakens the coating.

## Cleaning

- Wipe it with a soft brush or sponge, lukewarm water and a little mild soap.
- **Never put it in a washing machine or tumble dryer**, and never use bleach or solvents. These strip the waterproof coating and aren't covered by the warranty.
- Rinse off all the soap, then dry as above.

## Zips and buckles

- Brush sand out of zips with an old toothbrush.
- If a zip gets stiff, rub a little candle wax or zip lubricant along the teeth. Never force it.
- Keep buckles free of sand so they click shut fully.

## Using the roll-top

On the DryTrek and Roll-Top backpacks, fold the top over at least three times before you clip the buckle. Fewer folds let water in. These backpacks are rated IPX6: they keep out heavy rain and splashes, but they are not made to go under water. For that, use a dry bag inside.

## Storing it

Store it clean, completely dry and loosely packed, out of direct sun. A damp backpack left in a cupboard during the monsoon grows mould in days.

## If something breaks

Seams, zips and buckles that fail in normal use within a year of delivery are covered by the [warranty](warranty-claims).
