"Waterproof" means different things on different products. This page explains the numbers we use.

## Fabric: millimetres of water column

For jackets and tents we give a number in millimetres (mm), also called hydrostatic head. Picture a tall tube standing on the fabric and filling with water: the rating is how high the water can rise before drops come through. 10,000 mm means a column of water 10 metres high.

| Rating | What it handles | Our products |
|---|---|---|
| 1,500 mm | Light drizzle and short showers | None (we don't sell anything below 3,000 mm) |
| 3,000 mm | Steady rain for an hour or two | Tent flysheets (Compact, Solo and Family tents) |
| 5,000 mm | Rain for most of a day | Coastal Light Shell; tent floors |
| 10,000 mm | Heavy rain for hours | Monsoon Rain Jacket |
| 20,000 mm | All-day monsoon downpours, with straps pressing on your shoulders | Storm Pro Jacket |

Why do tent floors need more than flysheets? Kneeling or sitting on a floor pushes water through it, so the floor needs a higher rating than a flysheet that rain only falls on.

## Bags and pouches: IP ratings

Bags and pouches use an IP rating instead. The second digit is about water:

| Rating | What it means | Our products |
|---|---|---|
| IPX4 | Splashes from any direction | Monsoon Commuter Backpack |
| IPX6 | Strong jets of water, heavy rain | DryTrek and Roll-Top backpacks, roll-top dry bags |
| IPX8 | Under water: our pouches are tested at 2 m for 30 minutes | Phone pouches, waist pouch, document pouch |

Nothing on this page is a promise for diving: a phone pouch is for rain, boats and swimming at the surface.

## Keeping the rating

Ratings fall as the outer coating wears. Wash jackets gently and re-proof them once a season. See [Caring for your waterproof backpack](backpack-care).
